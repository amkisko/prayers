## Effects

### Contrary pass recorded before the claim ledger

The following attack log was written before constructing the main constructive ledger. The supplied proposal had already been read, so parent-prompt contamination remains.

- Passive documentation can outperform a skill if discovery fails; seek a counterexample to dynamic-skill superiority.
- A five-stage diagnostic model may be a local synthesis; require a named paper and passage for the attribution.
- Three repeats, 30-80 tasks, ten fixtures, and a three-module ceiling are not automatically adequate or optimal.
- Package availability changes metadata, information, executable resources, length, and selection work. Separate package effects from claims about the mechanism.
- Conditioning on activation or adherence can select on post-treatment behavior. Keep non-activation in the assigned-arm analysis.
- Deterministic verifiers can omit intended behavior and side effects; reproducibility does not establish construct validity.
- Loaded bytes do not prove use. Missing trace evidence may reflect logging, truncation, caching, or retained context.
- Generic rules can help when they add missing facts. Evidence of harm in one workload does not establish universal harm.
- Repeated task trials are clustered; an ordinary paired-binary test does not automatically handle them.
- Version labels and prose labels do not determine causal effect.
- Inspect actual check paths before attributing model-performance evidence to repository tests or context budgets.

## Source

### S01: SkillsBench

- Author: Xiangyi Li et al.
- Title: SkillsBench: Benchmarking How Well Agent Skills Work Across Diverse Tasks.
- Date and type: arXiv preprint v4, 2026-06-14; original submission 2026-02-13.
- URL: https://arxiv.org/html/2602.12670v4
- Locators: section 4, equation 1; section 6.1; Appendix F.1-F.2; Appendix G; Appendix N.
- Paraphrase: paired skill-access evaluation, task-macro aggregation, and normalized gain. Quantity/length groups compare different tasks. The paper acknowledges stronger matched-length controls remain needed; its plotted main rate intervals use Wald binomial intervals.
- Limit: no prayers treatment, no randomized same-task bundle-size optimum. Task filtering and curated artifacts constrain transfer. The main review does not adopt its interval construction automatically.

### S02: AGENTIF

- Author: Yunjia Qi et al.
- Title: AGENTIF: Benchmarking Instruction Following of Large Language Models in Agentic Scenarios.
- Date and type: NeurIPS 2025 Datasets and Benchmarks proceedings paper.
- URL: https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/file/51bb3a8a33610a25aae074bfc51b1b1f-Paper-Datasets_and_Benchmarks_Track.pdf
- Locator: section 3.4, printed page 6; equations for CSR and ISR.
- Paraphrase: CSR counts satisfied applicable constraints; ISR counts fully satisfied instructions. Untriggered conditional constraints are excluded. Verification can be code-based, model-based, or hybrid.
- Limit: instruction-following evaluation, not proof of long-running tool-agent task success or prayers benefit.

### S03: Vercel documentation experiment

- Author: Jude Gao, Vercel.
- Title: AGENTS.md outperforms skills in our agent evals.
- Date and type: 2026-01-27, original practitioner report.
- URL: https://vercel.com/blog/agents-md-outperforms-skills-in-our-agent-evals
- Locators: Skills weren't being triggered reliably; Explicit instructions helped, but wording was fragile; The results surprised us.
- Paraphrase: the reported Next.js conditions score 53% for baseline and default skill, 79% with explicit invocation guidance, and 100% with an AGENTS.md documentation index. Invocation wording changed behavior.
- Limit: a narrow framework/version evaluation, not a universal passive-context result. The article does not give enough trial-level uncertainty and independent replication evidence to generalize the percentages to prayers.

### S04: Repository context evaluation

- Author: Thibaud Gloaguen et al.
- Title: Evaluating AGENTS.md: Are Repository-Level Context Files Helpful for Coding Agents?
- Date and type: arXiv preprint v3, 2026-09-29; original submission 2026-02-12.
- URL: https://arxiv.org/html/2602.11988v3
- Locators: sections 4.2-4.4; section 5; Appendix B.1.
- Paraphrase: context files did not generally improve task success in the studied settings, while average inference cost increased by over 20%. The paper distinguishes task resolution from supplying non-standard practices.
- Limit: generated and developer context files in specified coding workloads; neither the full prayers catalog nor all instruction objectives. Observed adherence without a success gain does not establish that adherence never matters.

### S05: SWE-Skills-Bench

- Author: Tingxu Han et al.
- Title: SWE-Skills-Bench: Do Agent Skills Actually Help in Real-World Software Engineering?
- Date and type: arXiv preprint v2, 2026-10-27; original submission 2026-03-16.
- URL: https://arxiv.org/html/2603.15401v2
- Locators: abstract; sections 3.2-3.4; section 5; Appendix A.1.
- Paraphrase: the revised study uses 98 skills, skill-derived requirements, and explicit initial invocation. It reports 92 without significant effect and average pass ratios of 76.6% and 76.9% without/with skills.
- Limit: absence of statistical significance is not equivalence or zero effect. Requirements and verifiers are synthesized from skills; the paper acknowledges distribution and verifier limitations. Older summaries about 49 skills and +1.2% describe another version and were not used as current results.

### S06: Skill representation and retrieval

- Author: Zhiyuan Jiang et al.
- Title: Demystifying Agent Skills: Why They Work-Until They Don't.
- Date and type: arXiv preprint v1, 2026-08-14.
- URL: https://arxiv.org/html/2608.14036v1
- Locators: sections 3.3, 5.5, and 7; Appendix A.7-A.9.
- Paraphrase: the study separates matched experience representations from retrieval and actual execution. Exact annotated-skill invocation and downstream success are not interchangeable.
- Limit: labeled trajectory mechanisms involve judgment; benchmarks and artifacts overlap other research. Transfer of a mechanism to prayers remains an inference requiring intervention tests.

### S07: Skill-Use

- Author: Jinyi Han et al.
- Title: Skill-Use: Can LLMs Actually Use Skills in Agentic Harnesses?
- Date and type: arXiv preprint v1, 2026-08-05.
- URL: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.04828v1
- Locators: section 3, definitions 1-3, printed page 4; sections 4.2 and 4.4.
- Paraphrase: the benchmark explicitly separates Trigger, Compliance, and Boundary under progressive disclosure and compares native with preloaded injection.
- Limit: its composite gates credit on triggering; that score is a defined use metric, not a general total task-utility estimand. Post-trigger comparisons need their stated scope.

### S09: Web development skills

- Author: Ziyue Yang and Fan Ding, as listed on the arXiv abstract page.

- Paraphrase: matched injected-skill and irrelevant-length controls distinguish prompt-length distraction from content effects; component ablations evaluate content slices.
- Limit: a web-development task chain with deliberate injection, not natural skill discovery. Its Pass@2 and cost effects should not be pooled with single-run acceptance rates or other benchmark gains.

### S11: Official output-evaluation guidance

### S12: One-factor-at-a-time limitation

- Institution: NIST/SEMATECH e-Handbook of Statistical Methods.
- Title: One variable at a time, section 5.2.1.2.
- Date and type: undated official statistical reference, accessed 2026-11-16.
- URL: https://www.itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/pri/section2/pri212.htm
- Locator: The OFAT approach does not take interactions into account.
- Paraphrase: varying one factor while fixing others can miss factor interactions.
- Limit: general design guidance. A factorial experiment is useful when interactions matter; this does not require a large factorial grid for every prayers comparison.

### S13: McNemar implementation contract

- Institution: statsmodels developers.
- Title: statsmodels.stats.contingency_tables.mcnemar.
- Date and type: official stable API documentation, served as version 0.15.0 on access.
- URL: https://www.statsmodels.org/stable/generated/statsmodels.stats.contingency_tables.mcnemar.html
- Locators: Parameters, Returns, and exact-binomial behavior.
- Paraphrase: input is a two-by-two contingency table; the exact test uses discordant paired counts and a binomial distribution.
- Limit: the API does not validate whether repeated rows are independent scientific units. No statsmodels dependency was added or executed in this work.

### S14: Few-run uncertainty

- Author: Rishabh Agarwal et al.
- Title: Deep Reinforcement Learning at the Edge of the Statistical Precipice.
- Date and type: NeurIPS 2021 paper; arXiv v2 dated 2021-10-05.
- URL: https://arxiv.org/html/2108.13264v2
- Locators: sections 2-4.1; Appendix A.5.
- Paraphrase: finite tasks and runs can make benchmark point-estimate comparisons unreliable; interval estimation and resampling should reflect the evaluation structure.
- Limit: deep reinforcement learning, not a validated prayers sample-size prescription. Applying its uncertainty principles to tool-agent experiments is a methodological inference.

### S15: Model judges

- Author: Lianmin Zheng et al.
- Title: Judging LLM-as-a-Judge with MT-Bench and Chatbot Arena.
- Date and type: NeurIPS 2023 Datasets and Benchmarks paper; arXiv v4 dated 2023-12-24.
- URL: https://arxiv.org/html/2306.05685v4
- Locators: sections 3.3-3.4 and 4.
- Paraphrase: model judges can approximate human preferences but show position, verbosity, self-enhancement, and reasoning limitations.
- Limit: conversational preference evaluation. Agreement from this population does not validate a judge for authorization boundaries or engineering correctness.

### S16: Post-treatment selection

- Author: Jacob M. Montgomery, Brendan Nyhan, and Michelle Torres.
- Title: How Conditioning on Posttreatment Variables Can Ruin Your Experiment and What to Do about It.
- Date and type: American Journal of Political Science, 2018, volume 62(3), pages 760-775; first published 2018-03-30.
- DOI: https://doi.org/10.1111/ajps.12357
- Open author manuscript: https://cpb-us-e1.wpmucdn.com/sites.dartmouth.edu/dist/5/2293/files/2021/03/post-treatment-bias.pdf
- Locators: abstract and the analyses of subsetting/conditioning on post-treatment variables.
- Paraphrase: excluding or conditioning observations using treatment-affected variables can bias causal estimates.
- Limit: the application to activated skill runs is this review's design inference, not an agent experiment in the paper. Conditional descriptive metrics remain useful if clearly labeled.
